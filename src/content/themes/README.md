Keep new study prose in this directory so its licensing boundary is explicit.
Code and presentation components remain MIT. Do not copy previously MIT-licensed
summaries or third-party commentary here and claim exclusive non-commercial terms.
Bible quotations and datasets retain their own terms; these studies link to passages
rather than reproducing translation text.

Each theme needs a unique slug and title. Top-level themes have `parentSlug: null`;
sub-themes identify their parent using `parentSlug`. Keep related study links within
the published collection. Studies can add an overview, key passages, explanatory
sections, contextual language notes and reflection questions. Themes awaiting
content may leave those fields empty. Sections may include `letterGroups` with
names, Hebrew letters, symbolic connections and `entries` for an interactive chart.
Each entry supplies the letter, name, meaning, usage and a reflection prompt.
Keep letter-specific associations distinct from shared family associations. A study may include `sources`
with a bibliographic `citation` and a `detail` note, displayed once at the end
of the study. Summarise source material in original prose and distinguish source
claims from the study’s own biblical comparisons. Third-party sources retain
their own rights.
Write original explanations, read passages in context, and acknowledge significant
differences between Christian interpretations rather than presenting them as settled.
